Field Exercise

Test Your Positioning

A 20-Minute Test for Whether Your Identity Is Actually Clear

July 2026 · 20 minute exercise

The Exercise

Set a timer for 20 minutes.

Without mentioning your genre, equipment, influences, or accomplishments, answer this question in one sentence:

Why would someone remember you after experiencing your work once?

Now send that same question to three people who genuinely know your work.

Ask them to answer independently.

Don't explain.

Don't give hints.

Just collect their responses.

Compare the answers.

  • Do they describe the same artist?
  • Do they use similar language?
  • Do they identify the same strengths?
  • Do they describe the same emotional experience?

If the answers are wildly different…

You don't have a promotion problem.

You have a positioning problem.

Reflection

Positioning isn't about controlling perception.

It's about creating enough clarity that people naturally arrive at a similar conclusion.

When your positioning is strong, your audience begins describing your work the way you hoped they would, without ever being instructed to.

That's when your identity starts becoming recognizable.

Positioning Canvas

Complete these prompts before investing in promotion.

  • I help people experience…
  • The artists I'm most often compared to are…
  • What makes me different is…
  • The feeling I want people to leave with is…
  • The kinds of opportunities I'm trying to create are…
  • The opportunities I am intentionally not pursuing are…
Sentient Principle
If you can't clearly position yourself, your audience will do it for you.
You may not like the result.
Field Notes
  • Clarity beats cleverness.
  • Different is more memorable than better.
  • Positioning is a strategic decision, not a design exercise.
  • Every “yes” to one audience is a “no” to another.
  • Recognition compounds faster than attention.
