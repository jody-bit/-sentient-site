Mount Clemens Electronic Music Festival

2026 Case Study / Impact White Paper

Mount Clemens, Michigan · August 22, 2026
Executive Summary

In 2026, Mount Clemens launched its inaugural electronic music festival: a free, community-focused event bringing approximately 20 performers to three stages throughout downtown Mount Clemens.

Sentient Productions joined the project after its initial creative direction had already begun.

An original poster existed. Multiple organizers and creative contributors were involved. Individual stages developed their own requirements. Community partners and sponsors required visibility. The festival needed to speak simultaneously to established electronic-music audiences, families, children and people encountering the culture for the first time.

Sentient’s challenge was therefore larger than creating promotional artwork.

It was to transform an emerging collection of creative ideas, stakeholder requirements and event information into a coherent festival system capable of functioning across screens, streets, stages and audiences.

The resulting work encompassed brand development, creative direction, festival communications, digital experience, environmental applications, physical artifacts, audience development and measurement.

On August 22, the system became a real-world experience.

Available Placer.ai reporting recorded approximately 6,300 visits to the defined Downtown Mount Clemens market, a 51.5% year-over-year increase. The report shows downtown activity building through the afternoon toward an evening peak of approximately 2,000 people.

Separately, MCEMF’s digital ecosystem generated more than 1,100 active website users, approximately 1,800 views of the primary festival page, 777 Artists-page views and 7,654 tracked website events/interactions during the reporting period surrounding the inaugural festival.

01 · The Opportunity

Electronic music, embedded in a downtown.

Electronic music culture is frequently presented through nightlife, clubs, late hours and adult-oriented festivals.

Mount Clemens Electronic Music Festival was attempting something different.

The inaugural event was conceived as a free, community-focused electronic music experience embedded within downtown Mount Clemens.

Electronic-music fans would share the environment with families, children, downtown visitors and people who might encounter the culture simply because something interesting was happening in their city.

That created a compelling strategic question:

How do you make electronic music accessible to a broader community without sanitizing the culture that makes it compelling?

There was also a second challenge.

Sentient wasn’t starting with a blank canvas.

An original festival poster and emerging visual direction existed before Sentient became involved. Instead of erasing that history, the existing material became a creative constraint.

The task became identifying the visual DNA worth preserving and building enough structure around it to support an actual festival.

02 · The Real Design Problem

The challenge wasn’t creating an identity. It was maintaining one.

A festival isn’t a single artifact.

It is an ecosystem of artists, organizers, stages, sponsors, partners, websites, social platforms, signs, physical spaces and audiences.

MCEMF included approximately 20 performers across three stages, each contributing another layer of information and identity.

The collaborative production environment also contained different creative perspectives, operational priorities and levels of design experience.

Stage-specific sponsor and partner identification added another requirement. Sentient was asked to accommodate these identities while preserving the festival’s overarching visual integrity.

Rather than trying to make every participant design identically, Sentient needed to create a system flexible enough to contain difference.

That shifted the work from graphic design toward creative systems thinking.

03 · The Strategy

Sentient developed the festival’s creative direction around a simple principle:

Electronic music belongs to everyone.

The visual identity needed credibility within electronic culture without visually announcing that newcomers weren’t invited.

Instead of relying exclusively on the darker, industrial language frequently associated with electronic nightlife, MCEMF embraced energy, movement, color and play.

The evolving visual system combined:

  • Sunset magenta · Festival purple · Deep navy · Record-label orange · Warm cream
  • Bold typography · Illustrated characters · Pixel motifs · Electronic references · Skyline elements · Equalizer forms · Retro arcade influences

The resulting world could communicate electronic music without communicating members only.

04 · From Poster to System

The inherited festival poster became the starting point rather than the finished identity.

Sentient identified elements capable of carrying forward and developed a more flexible visual language around them.

As the project expanded, hierarchy became essential.

The system needed to distinguish between:

  • Festival
  • Stage
  • Artist
  • Partner
  • Information

That hierarchy allowed new content to enter the system without requiring a new design language every time something changed.

The identity ultimately expanded across the festival poster, artist lineup, stage identities, social communications, website, partner integration, environmental graphics, physical collateral and collectible artifacts.

Artwork became infrastructure.

The System in Practice

05 · Designing Inside a Multi-Stakeholder System

Participation with coherence.

The realities of producing a community festival introduced constraints that don’t exist in a design studio.

Different stakeholders brought different priorities.

Artists needed visibility.

Organizers needed information communicated.

Stages needed differentiation.

Sponsors and partners needed recognition.

Audiences needed clarity.

The city environment imposed physical constraints.

And all of it still needed to look like the same festival.

Sentient’s creative-direction role therefore required balancing participation with coherence.

06 · Building the Digital Festival

The festival’s central digital destination.

The website became MCEMF’s central digital destination.

08 · From Screen to Street

Designed to survive contact with the real world.

The brand was designed to survive contact with the real world.

Sentient extended the visual system into stage graphics, environmental concepts, printed collateral and physical artifacts.

This mattered because MCEMF wasn’t taking place inside a purpose-built festival ground.

Downtown Mount Clemens was the venue.

Existing streets, businesses, passages and spaces needed to become part of the experience.

09 · A One-Inch Festival Artifact

Not every piece of identity needed to be large.

Sentient created a compact secondary MCEMF mark inspired by the visual vocabulary of electronic components.

The geometric microchip-style design reduced the festival initials into a mark capable of working at approximately one inch.

It became a physical button.

Buttons were distributed to participating DJs and hidden throughout festival planters as small Easter eggs for attendees to discover.

10 · Designing the Invitation Wider

A larger doorway, not a different culture.

The festival’s audience strategy was visible in the people occupying the environment.

Families and children weren’t isolated in a separate experience. They shared downtown with DJs, electronic-music fans, visitors and people discovering the event by proximity.

That reinforced one of the project’s central principles:

Accessibility doesn’t require dilution.

Electronic culture didn’t need to become something else to welcome a broader audience.

The doorway simply needed to become larger.

14 · The Outcome

Coherent, recognizable and real.

Sentient wasn’t brought in to invent the Mount Clemens Electronic Music Festival.

It was brought in to help make an emerging idea coherent, recognizable and real.

What began with an existing poster and a collection of creative ideas developed into a system capable of connecting approximately 20 performers, three stages, sponsors, partners, digital communications and physical environments.

The brand existed on screens.

Then on signs.

Then on stages.

Then on buttons hidden in planters.

And ultimately in a downtown occupied by thousands of visits over the course of the day.

That progression is the project.

16 · What the Project Demonstrated

MCEMF became an early demonstration of the way Sentient approaches creative work.

Not as isolated deliverables.

As connected systems.

A poster affects expectations.

A website affects discovery.

Information architecture affects participation.

A sponsor requirement affects hierarchy.

A physical environment affects behavior.

An artifact affects memory.

And measurement determines whether any of those decisions can eventually become knowledge.

Sentient’s role sat at the intersection of those things.

Brand became experience.
Experience became participation.
Participation became signal.
Conclusion

The inaugural Mount Clemens Electronic Music Festival began as an ambitious community experiment: put electronic music into downtown Mount Clemens, remove the admission barrier and invite the city inside.

Sentient Productions helped create the connective tissue that allowed that experiment to present itself as one experience.

The result was not simply a collection of design deliverables.

It was the beginning of a recognizable festival world spanning identity, information, digital experience, physical space, stakeholders and audience.

And on August 22, 2026, people stepped into it.
